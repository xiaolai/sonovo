# Tidewater — Ep. 01: The Harbor at Six

**Narration draft**

## Cold open

The harbor at six is a held breath.

A gull lifts off the pilings before the light reaches them. Ropes knock
against the masts. Somewhere past the breakwater, an engine turns over and
then thinks better of it.

## Act one

Mara walks the quay with a paper cup and a list she does not read. She knows
the order of things here: the ice truck first, then the fuel truck, then the
boats that go out and the ones that stay.

The ones that stay are the story.

## Act two

At the co-op, the ledgers disagree with the weather. The season opened late.
The catch came in thin. By the time the light is full, the room has split into
two conversations — the one about the boats and the one about the money.

Mara listens to both. She has learned that the harbor argues in two directions
at once, and that the argument is how it stays afloat.

## Close

By seven, the fog has burned off. The water is the color of a coin. The boats
that went out are small now, and the ones that stayed are smaller still.

Mara folds her list and puts it away.

> The harbor at six is a held breath. By seven, it has decided to breathe.
